7.11 · The tool

The multi-tool is slow, but it reaches cuts other saws can't

How it moves

The blade swings back and forth in a tiny arc, thousands of times a minute, instead of spinning or stroking.

Good at

Flush cuts against a surface, small plunge cuts into the middle of a panel, and sanding into corners.

Not for

Long straight cuts, which go on a saw built for them. Aluminum tube goes on the metal chop saw (7.5).

7.11 · Blades

Use only blades that fit our mount, and pick bi-metal when there might be metal

The mount

An accessory has to fit our tool's mount and sit fully down on the pins. Brands don't all fit each other.

Never modify

Don't drill out or file a blade to make it fit, and don't use one the manual doesn't recommend. A loose blade can work its way off.

Look it over

No cracks, and teeth not worn flat.

Which blade

Wood blades are for clean wood. A bi-metal blade handles wood with hidden nails or staples, and soft metal. Wood blade teeth strip off on metal.

7.11 · Accessory change

Take the battery out for every accessory change, and let the blade cool first

1

Battery out

Every time. The speed dial is not an off switch.

2

Let it cool

The blade gets hot from cutting and sanding.

3

Seat it

Fit the accessory fully down on the pins and tighten it the way the manual shows.

4

Battery back

Only after the accessory is seated and tight.

7.11 · Hands

The blade cuts skin, so clamp the work and keep your hands out of its path

The myth

Some people think a blade that only moves a tiny bit can't cut skin. False. The teeth move thousands of times a minute and cut fingers as easily as plastic.

Hands clear

Keep your hands out of the blade's path, both in front of the part and behind it.

Clamp it

Clamp the work to the bench or put it in a vise, with the part being cut off hanging past the edge. Never hold what you're cutting.

Why

Vibration walks a hand-held part around, and your hand goes with it into the blade.

Bank: 7.11 Oscillating Multi-Tool; FIRST Safety Manual §3.2.1

7.11 · Cutting

Start plunge cuts slow and ease in, and slow down when plastic starts to melt

Plunge cuts

Start at a low speed, line the blade up, and ease it in. Let the tool do the work, and rock it slightly if it slows.

Don't force it

Forcing overheats the blade and the part, and the blade can jump out of the slot. Prying bends the blade.

Plastic melting

Heat melts plastic like polycarbonate and gums up the blade. Turn the speed down and push more lightly, so it cuts instead of rubs.

7.11 · On the robot

Before cutting anything on the robot, check behind it and unplug the robot battery

Look first

Check behind and under the panel for wires, pneumatic tubing, and the battery before you cut.

Unplug the battery

Disabled doesn't mean safe. Unplug the robot battery.

Protect it

Cover the electronics so chips can't get in. Hold the tool by its insulated grip if the blade could hit a wire.

Better yet

Take the panel off the robot and clamp it on the bench.

7.11 · PPE

The blade and the part get hot, so let them cool, and gear up for dust and noise

Heat

Friction heats the blade fast, and the part too. Let both cool before you touch them or change the blade.

PPE

Safety glasses. A dust mask if it's dusty. Hearing protection for longer jobs.

Prep

Sleeves rolled and nothing loose, on every job, short or long. No gloves at spinning or oscillating tools.

Spot the problem

A part held in fingers, a blade modified to fit, or no glasses: stop and fix it. Plunge cuts are what it's for.
