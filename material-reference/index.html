Material reference

All ratings are 1–5. 5/5 is always the most favorable for FRC use — strongest, lightest, cheapest, easiest to make, best sliding surface, etc. See the scale table below.

Rating1/5 — worst for FRC5/5 — best for FRC
Strength Very weak — fails under typical robot loadsMDF crumbles, PLA brackets snap, HDPE sheet buckles Handles extreme loads and impactsSteel 1018, titanium, CFRP, 7075-T6 aluminum
Light weight Very heavy — adds significant robot massSteel plate (~7.8 g/cm³), iron pipe, Unistrut Very light — minimal weight penaltyCFRP (~1.6 g/cm³), aluminum 6061 (~2.7 g/cm³), HDPE (~0.95 g/cm³)
Ductility Brittle — shatters or fractures with no warningAcrylic, CFRP sheet, MDF, PLA — all snap suddenly Highly plastic — bends substantially before failureMild steel, polycarbonate, HDPE, copper tube
Fabrication ease Specialist-only — needs advanced tooling or outsourcingTitanium (work-hardens), DMLS parts (outsourced only), CFRP (diamond tooling, PPE) Trivial — hand tools, no special setup, hardware storePVC pipe (pipe cutter), wood (hand saw), HDPE (utility knife), PLA (FDM printer)
Cost-effectiveness Very expensive — high per-part or per-kg costDMLS titanium ($300–1000+/part), CFRP sheet ($50–200+/ft²), titanium stock Very affordable — cheap and widely availablePVC pipe (~$5/length), MDF, acrylic, PLA filament (~$20/kg)
Friction / sliding Poor sliding surface — high friction, wears or damagesPLA, acrylic, raw wood — game pieces drag and hang up Excellent sliding surface — low friction, wear-resistantHDPE is #1 (best stock sliding plastic), Delrin, nylon stock
Heat resistance Deforms or fails at surprisingly low temperaturesPLA softens at ~60°C, HDPE at ~80°C — both fail near motors Handles high heat — well above any FRC operating tempSteels, titanium, iron — melt points above 1400°C
