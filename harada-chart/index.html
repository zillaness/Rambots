Harada Chart

How this works

Step 1: a SMART goal

One goal for your whole 2027 season, written so you can actually check it. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. "Get better at CAD" is a wish. "Design a mechanism that makes it onto the 2027 robot by our first regional" is a goal.

Step 2: the Harada chart

Takashi Harada, a Japanese track coach, built this 9×9 grid to turn one big goal into things you can do this week. Your goal goes in the middle, 8 pillars support it, and each pillar gets up to 8 small actions. At 16, Shohei Ohtani filled one out. One of his pillars was luck (運), and its actions were things like picking up trash and greeting people.

You don't need all 64

  1. Write your SMART goal and send it to the chart.
  2. Name as many pillars as you can.
  3. Add 2 or 3 actions under the pillars that matter most. Good actions are small and checkable: "drive the practice bot 30 min a week," not "get better at driving."

The goal is big and season-long. The actions are the small steps, like the skills on the team's 30/60/90 list. Come back and add more over the season. Save your file each time.
