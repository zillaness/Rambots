The Acute Gusset Project

Design in Onshape  →  Laser cut wood  →  3D print  →  Assemble
Why is the angle acute? Because it's less than 90°... and small things are cute. 🙂
Your gusset must connect two sections of MaxTube at an angle strictly less than 90°. If your angle is 90° or more, we'll know you didn't read this.

Plate B — 3D Printed

3D printers consistently print holes smaller than designed. Use pitch as your correction factor:

The ±½ Pitch Rule
Clearance hole (bolt slides through): design Ø = nominal + ½ × pitch
Interference fit (bolt press-fits in): design Ø = nominal − ½ × pitch
// Hint: the fastener callout tells you everything you need #10 → nominal major diameter (look it up — there's a formula) -32 → threads per inch (this gives you the pitch) // Once you have pitch, you can derive both hole sizes yourself. // Check your answer against a Starrett chart or clearance drill table.

Alternative Reference
Not sure about the ½ pitch shortcut? Look it up on a Starrett chart or any standard tap/clearance drill table. The rule gets you very close — the chart gets you exact.

Slicing Hint — Prevent Warping

Watch Out
This part has large flat surfaces and sharp corners — exactly the geometry most likely to give you trouble on the printer. Think about why corners lift, and what you might do in your slicer or part orientation to fight it before you hit print.
04

Onshape Checklist

  • Sketch your angle constraint first. Lock in the acute angle with a construction line before drawing anything else.
  • Sketch and extrude Plate A (laser cut). Flat profile, ready for DXF export.
  • All holes on Plate A: correct clearance size for a #10. Use the Hole feature — not manual circles.
  • Sketch and extrude Plate B (3D print). Same gusset shape, same hole positions.
  • Alternate the hole types on Plate B: clearance (your calculated size) and interference (your calculated size).
  • Export Plate A as DXF for the laser cutter.
  • Export Plate B as STL for the 3D printer. Start the print early.
  • Bonus: Build an Assembly in Onshape, mate both plates to MaxTube, and verify before you manufacture.
Before You Export
Double-check hole diameters. Wrong size on the laser cut plate = bolt won't fit and you can't un-cut wood. Wrong interference hole = reprint, which costs time.
