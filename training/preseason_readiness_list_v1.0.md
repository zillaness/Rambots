# Rambots Preseason Readiness List (Draft)

What a student should know or be able to do before kickoff (Jan 9). Structured as a baseline every member hits, plus a layer for each subteam.

Items are written as outcomes ("can do X"), not sessions ("attended X"), so each one can be checked.

- `[D26]` 2026 season debrief responses
- `[SAC]` 2025 Sacramento Regional debrief
- `[BI]` Business Improvements doc
- `[BD]` BD knowledge download
- `[+]` My addition, not from a source

## Baseline: Every Member

### Safety

- [ ] Wears safety glasses in the shop without being reminded
- [ ] Has passed the tool quiz for every tool they use; no tool use without certification after Oct 1 `[GT]` `[BC]`
- [ ] Can explain what could go wrong before starting a task (cause and effect, not just rules) `[GT]`: rated "still lacking proactive thinking" at Sacramento 2025
- [ ] Knows basic first aid and CPR; flagged in both the 2025 and 2026 debriefs `[D25]` `[D26]`
- [ ] Knows where the first aid kit is and what's in it `[SAC]`
- [ ] Has completed a safety refresher this season, including returning members `[D25]`: one respondent said refreshers should be mandatory

### Shop and Tools

- [ ] Hand tool fundamentals, covered tool by tool, before any power tool `[GT]` `[D26]`: "still needs hand tool work" after 2025
- [ ] Knows where each tool lives and puts it back `[GT]` `[D26]`
- [ ] Can run the cleanup checklist and leave the room reset `[GT]` `[TL]`
- [ ] Can identify common fasteners by thread and size (10-32, 1/4-20, 8-32, metric) `[BC]`
- [ ] Has been in the workshop early in preseason, business members included `[D26]`

### How the Team Runs

- [ ] Knows the season calendar, competition travel, and time commitment (covered at Sep 9 onboarding) `[TL]`
- [ ] Knows the meeting structure: housekeeping, learning objective, something satisfying `[BD]`
- [ ] Can find their own task in Coda and update its status without being asked `[BI]` `[GT]` `[BD]`: "students did not receive or update their own tasks"
- [ ] Can give a standup update (what I did, what's next, what's blocking me) `[GT]`
- [ ] Knows how to submit a purchase request `[D26]`
- [ ] Knows which Discord channels to use for what `[BI]`

### FIRST and FRC Basics

- [ ] Can explain what FRC is, how a season works, and what happens at kickoff and during the 6-week build `[TL]` `[BC]`: the FRC intro got bumped repeatedly during boot camp
- [ ] Can describe the engineering design cycle `[BC]`: also bumped
- [ ] Knows they can be judged or scouted at any time, and can give a 30-second pitch about the team `[GT]` `[BD]`: "low awareness of when being judged or scouted"
- [ ] Can explain Gracious Professionalism and what it looks like in the pit `[D25]`
- [ ] Knows the main community resources: Chief Delphi, FRCDesign.org, The Blue Alliance `[D26]` `[BC]` `[+]`
- [ ] Knows at least the basics of what other subteams do `[GT]`: "low knowledge of anything outside one's responsibilities"

### Documentation

- [ ] Records work and reasoning in the engineering notebook or build binder, not just results `[GT]`: documentation was the major weakness of 2025

## Build / Mechanical

### CAD

- [ ] Onshape account set up and linked to the team workspace `[TL]` `[BC]`
- [ ] Completed FRCDesign.org Stage 1A (plates, gussets, motor mounting) `[BC]`
- [ ] Completed Stage 1B (motors, shafts, bearings) `[BC]` `[6/6]`: did not finish in one day during boot camp
- [ ] Can take a part from CAD to laser cut to assembled, start to finish `[6/6]`
- [ ] Can plan wire paths in CAD alongside the mechanism `[GT]`
- [ ] `[+]` Can redo a Stage 1 exercise unassisted in October. The 2025 note says summer CAD "seems to not have stuck," so this is a retention check, not a new skill.

### Fabrication

- [ ] 3D printing: can send a job, troubleshoot a failed print, and upload to MakerWorld `[TL]`
- [ ] Can pick a filament and slicer settings for a load-bearing part `[GT]`: printed parts "still underperformed" in 2025
- [ ] Laser cutter: can load and run a job, and never leaves it unattended `[BC]` `[6/6]`
- [ ] Has done the Preview Day rotations: vinyl cutter, laser cutter, 3D printer, heat press `[TL]`
- [ ] Passed the power tool safety test `[TL]`
- [ ] Can drill and tap a hole correctly `[BC]`
- [ ] Knows the basics of material selection `[BC]`
- [ ] Knows where rivets do and don't belong on a structure `[GT]`: "rivets in bad places" in 2024

### Electrical

- [ ] Can wire an FRC control system, including CAN bus `[TL]` `[GT]`: CAN failures persisted in 2024
- [ ] Knows pneumatics basics `[TL]`
- [ ] Knows motor anatomy `[TL]`
- [ ] Can solder a clean, strain-relieved joint `[TL]` `[GT]`
- [ ] Can check battery health with the Battery Beak `[TL]`
- [ ] Routes and secures wiring to a standard (wire management QC book) `[GT]` `[D25]`
- [ ] Can read and update the robot's wiring diagram `[GT]`: "documentation and diagrams could still improve"

### Programming

- [ ] Has written and deployed robot code using WPILib, not just general programming `[D25]`: preseason was asked to be "more robot-specific"
- [ ] Can build a basic path in PathPlanner `[D25]`
- [ ] Saves and tags AdvantageScope logs after every drive practice, and can sync them with video `[GT]`
- [ ] Can run the preflight and postflight checklists `[D26]` `[GT]`

## Drive Team and Strategy

- [ ] Has gone through real driver tryouts `[GT]`
- [ ] Has logged real practice reps, including non-ideal scenarios (defense, broken mechanism, bad start position) `[GT]`
- [ ] Can read the field and avoid wasted motion `[GT]`: rated "still poor" two years running
- [ ] Can make in-match calls quickly (observe, decide, act) `[GT]`
- [ ] Human player: knows the role and has practiced it `[D26]`
- [ ] Has played the Simulation Game, including alliance selection and drive team communication `[TL]`

## Scouting

- [ ] Can use the scouting app and knows what data matters `[GT]` `[SAC]`
- [ ] Understands how scouting data turns into a pick list `[GT]`
- [ ] Knows where the scouting process is documented `[GT]`

## Business

- [ ] Attended the business-specific onboarding session `[BI]`
- [ ] Has read the Business Bible `[BI]`
- [ ] Knows the grant tracker and sponsor database, and how to update them `[BI]`
- [ ] Financial basics: the accounts, reimbursement, restricted vs. unrestricted funds, what a W-9 is `[BI]`
- [ ] Knows the event cash handling process (starting float, who holds cash, post-event count) `[BI]`
- [ ] Has drafted one grant paragraph against a real prompt `[BI]`
- [ ] Has drafted and peer-reviewed a real sponsor email `[BI]`
- [ ] Has done one round of cold call role-play `[BI]`
- [ ] Has done Round 1 judge practice `[BI]`
- [ ] Knows the social media strategy and content calendar `[BI]`
- [ ] Can fill out a post-event debrief within 48 hours of an event `[BI]`
- [ ] Knows the rule: one business member in the pit at all times `[BD]`

## Leads Only

- [ ] Can run a planning meeting using the team's meeting guide (Lead, Decider, Note-taker, Timekeeper) `[+]` (from the meeting planning guide)
- [ ] Can onboard and pace a new or inconsistent member `[D26]`: a respondent asked to learn this directly
- [ ] Current on the leadership curriculum `[BD]`

## Open Questions

1. **Deadline.** Kickoff (Jan 9) or the start of the Kickoff Simulation (Oct 25)? `[+]` Testing readiness before the simulation would let the simulation surface gaps instead of carrying them into build season.
2. **Required vs. nice to have.** Which baseline items are hard gates? Tool certification already is.
3. **Who signs off.** Mentor, subteam lead, or self-check? The list only has teeth if someone verifies it.
4. **Electrical and programming.** A 2025 respondent asked to combine them. Keep them separate here or merge?
5. **Debrief ratings.** The 2026 debrief rates Safety, Preflight/Postflight Checklists, and Tool Knowledge/Training on a 1 to 5 scale. Confirm which end is good before citing those numbers.
